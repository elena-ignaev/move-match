# Evaluating the feedback

**Status:** the method has been tested on synthetic cases with known ground truth and on a few real
recordings. It has **not yet** been compared with a dance teacher's judgment. Until it has, the feedback is
labelled experimental in the app, and it shows measurements (seconds, degrees, which joint) instead of a score.

## Why not just report a score?

A number like "82% accurate" looks precise, but nothing yet shows that 82 means "nearly ready" to a teacher,
or that 82 vs. 78 is a real difference rather than tracking noise. The first scoring attempt
(`research/pose_score.py`) could not even satisfy three basic requirements at once on synthetic data (see
`research/calibrate_pose_k.py`). Separate, checkable statements are easier to verify and to disagree with.

## 1. Synthetic evaluation set (`evaluation/cases.py`, `evaluation/run_eval_set.py`)

Each case takes a synthetic choreography with realistic body proportions, renders a reference dancer and a
learner, and changes the learner in **one** controlled way. Because we know what was changed, we know what
correct feedback must say. Results: [`evaluation_synthetic_results.md`](evaluation_synthetic_results.md).

| Category | Cases | What correct feedback must say |
|---|---|---|
| Correct movement | correct, correct at 0.5×, slightly late (0.1 s), smaller dancer, farther from the camera, turn performed | on time, no moment flagged |
| Deliberately incorrect movement | right elbow 60° off; right elbow 25° off; mirror image without choosing mirror mode; missed turn; arm forward instead of sideways | the right joint/moment flagged, in the right time window |
| Delayed movement | 0.4 s late, 0.3 s early, 0.4 s late at 0.5× | the delay within ±0.07 s, posture not flagged |
| Missing body / poor tracking | legs out of frame, right arm occluded, person found in 30% of frames, dark room | the affected joints excluded, or feedback withheld with the reason |
| Mirroring | mirror image announced vs. unannounced | correct when announced, flagged when not |
| Single-camera depth error | correct dancing with ~6 cm and ~12 cm drifting depth error | no false flags |

**Current result: 20 of 21 behave as expected.** The miss is
`arm_forward_not_sideways`: reaching toward the camera instead of to the side changes no joint angle, so it
cannot be detected by this method. It stays in the set, marked as a known limitation, and the test suite
fails if it ever starts "passing" without the documentation being updated.

### What the evaluation set changed

* **Depth noise caused false flags.** Version 1.0 flagged correct dancing ("right knee 47° more bent") when
  simulated depth drifted by about 6 cm. Since 1.1, a flag needs the 2-D picture angle to agree with the 3-D
  angle, in the same direction. Both depth-noise cases now pass. The trade-off is sensitivity to errors that
  happen purely in depth.
* **The first synthetic skeleton was unrealistic.** `research/synthetic_dance` places limbs at random (10–20
  cm "forearms"), which exaggerates angle noise. The evaluation set now uses a standing pose with adult
  proportions.
* **A recording that stopped early** was compared at moments it never reached (found by the journey test).
  Such moments are now reported as "not recorded".
* **Tracking regions were too coarse.** Hiding one arm excluded both arms. Regions are now per side.

### What synthetic data cannot show

The cases are generated by the same person who wrote the method, so they test that it reacts to the right
things, not that it is right about real dancing. Real MediaPipe errors are only crudely imitated: label
swaps when facing away, loose clothing, motion blur, partial occlusion by furniture, and very fast moves.

## 2. Real recordings (2026-10-14)

Real MediaPipe output on the project's 40 s reference clip (one dancer, front view). "Recordings" were
cut from the same clip, so these check the **pipeline** (sync, timing, mirror handling) with real pose data.
They are not independent performances.

| Recording | Expected | MoveMatch said |
|---|---|---|
| Same section, in sync, 1× | on time, all close | on time (+0.001 s), 4/4 close, median 1° |
| Same section, starting 0.4 s late | 0.4 s late, all close | **0.401 s late**, 4/4 close |
| Same section at 0.5× (slowed footage, 0.5× playback) | on time | on time (−0.03 s), 3/3 close |
| Same section, horizontally flipped ("mirror image") | flagged | only 1 of 4 moments flagged: this section is fairly symmetric, so most mirrored poses look correct |
| Two prototype attempts (face filling the frame, body below the frame) | no feedback, with the reason | withheld: "no person found" and "upper body below the bottom edge of the frame" |

## 3. Comparison with a teacher: protocol (not yet done)

1. **Scope:** one dancer, a fixed camera at hip height 3–4 m away, the whole body visible, sections of 4–8 s.
2. **Recordings:** for 2–3 sections, record at least: a careful correct take; a take with a deliberate arm
   mistake; one with a deliberate leg mistake; one deliberately late; one partly out of frame; one in dim
   light; one by a dancer with different proportions; and one mirrored by mistake. Also record ordinary
   learner takes, which matter most.
3. **Export** the moments MoveMatch judged: `python evaluation/compare_with_teacher.py export`.
4. **Blind judgment:** a dance teacher or experienced dancer watches each replay with MoveMatch's columns
   hidden, and marks each moment *close / minor / review / cannot tell*, plus each take's timing
   (*early / on time / late*). Ideally two judges, to see how much humans agree with each other.
5. **Report:** `python evaluation/compare_with_teacher.py report --csv ... --out docs/evaluation_teacher_results.md`
   lists agreement counts and **every disagreement** with the teacher's notes.
6. **Act on it:** where the teacher flags and the app does not, check whether the joint was hidden, the
   mistake was in depth, or the threshold was too lenient. Where the app flags and the teacher does not,
   check tracking, and whether the teacher considers that difference style rather than error. Change
   thresholds only with that evidence, and bump `feedback.ALGORITHM_VERSION`.

## 4. Known limitations

See `docs/technical_decisions.md`, section 8. In short: depth is guessed from one camera; back-facing parts
can swap left and right; symmetric moves hide mirror mistakes; the thresholds (20°/35°, ±0.15 s, ±0.2 s
posture window) are provisional; timing assumes one roughly constant delay per section, plus drift detection.
